THE INCITING INCIDENT: The Creator's Friends
The cabin on Celeste Mountain had always been a sanctuary, a fortress of quiet solitude against a world that had become too loud. Now, it was a hospital ward.
A week had passed since the bunker incident. A week of sleeping in shifts, of rationing the emergency supplies Leo kept stocked, of speaking in the low, hushed tones of survivors. The seven of them had made the long, silent trek back from the park, the weight of their hollow victory a tangible thing in the cold mountain air.
They had brought their new, unexpected additions with them. Cora, the silent deer-woman, had proven to be unnervingly self-sufficient. She moved through the cabin with a quiet, observant grace, communicating only through the simple, elegant diagrams and brutally concise sentences she would sketch on a notepad she fabricated for herself. She was an engineer of reality, and she was watching everything, analyzing everyone. Her spherical companion, Wheatley, had, after a brief and terrified diagnostic, declared himself "mostly fine, with a slight chance of existential dread" and had appointed himself Yc’s deeply annoying personal assistant.
But the real patient, the true ghost at their quiet feast, was Airy.
He was… empty. The sarcastic, annoyed, foul-mouthed creator was gone. The focused, terrifying soldier with the flamethrower was gone. All that was left was a quiet, hollowed-out teenager who sat by the fire, wrapped in a thick wool blanket, staring into the flames with eyes that saw nothing. He didn't speak. He barely ate. The fight, the confession, and the final, terrible victory had cost him something profound. He had burned the monster out of his system, and now there was nothing left inside but the ash.
Mym watched him from across the room, the question still burning behind his own eyes. Was it fun? The reveal of Airy as the Author, the Killzone sniper, had fundamentally broken his own world. Every memory, every hard-won victory, was now tainted with the knowledge that it had all been a game. He didn’t know how to reconcile the hero he had become with the lab rat he had always been.
The fragile, wounded peace of their recovery was shattered by a sound that did not belong on their quiet mountain. It was the sound of a rattling, backfiring engine and, underneath it, the faint, unmistakable strains of a deeply inappropriate death metal song.
Everyone tensed. Leo was at the window in an instant, his face a grim mask. "We've got company," he said, his voice a low growl.
They looked outside. A beat-up, heavily modified van, painted a garish shade of purple and covered in what looked like hand-painted cat decals, was rattling its way up their secluded access road. But it wasn't just driving. The van seemed to flicker, its form glitching, de-rezzing for a split second into a green wireframe before snapping back into solid matter. It wasn’t driving up the mountain; it was forcing itself into their reality.
The van came to a screeching halt in the snow a few dozen feet from the cabin, its engine dying with a final, pathetic cough. The side door slid open with a deafening screech of protesting metal, and two figures spilled out into the snow.
The first, Finn, was tall and lanky, with a hyperactive energy that was visible even from a distance. The second, Gunner, was shorter, stockier, and radiated an aura of pure, unadulterated chaos.
They saw Airy, a solitary, blanket-wrapped figure visible through the cabin window. Their faces broke into huge, delighted grins.
"AIRY, MY MAN!" Gunner roared, his voice carrying easily through the glass. "WE SAW THE FLARE! HEARD YOU THREW ANOTHER REALITY-BENDING PARTY AND DIDN'T INVITE US! YOU WOUND ME! DEEPLY!"
They began trudging through the snow towards the cabin, their boots crunching loudly.
Mym looked at Airy. For the first time in a week, there was a flicker of something in his eyes. Not recognition. Not happiness. Just a profound, soul-deep weariness. The last thing a ghost wants is to be visited by the friends from his previous life.
Before anyone could decide whether to bar the door, Finn was already at the porch, kicking the snow off his boots. "Yo, Airy!" he yelled, his knuckles rapping against the wood of the door. "We bring snacks! And bad news! Mostly bad news, actually! The cosmic landlords are snitching on you!"
Leo held up a hand, stopping anyone else from moving. He crossed the room in three long strides, his expression a mask of cold suspicion. He didn't open the door. He stood beside it, his hand resting on the conceptual glock he couldn't summon in this reality, a ghost of a motion from a lifetime of tactical readiness.
"Identify yourselves," Leo's voice was a low growl, muffled by the thick wood of the door.
"We're friends of the sad-looking emo kid in the blanket!" Gunner's voice yelled back cheerfully. "We come in peace! Mostly! We brought jerky!"
Leo's eyes narrowed. He looked over at the silent, blanket-wrapped figure by the fire. Airy hadn't moved. He hadn't even looked up.
With a heavy sigh, Leo unbolted the door and opened it a crack, his body blocking the entrance. Finn and Gunner stood on the porch, a chaotic pair of mismatched energies, grinning like idiots.
"What do you want?" Leo asked, his voice leaving no room for games.
"We're here for him," Finn said, his smile faltering slightly under Leo's hostile gaze. He jerked a thumb towards Airy. "We're his friends. We're here to... help?" He glanced at Gunner, who nodded enthusiastically.
"Yeah! And we told you, we have bad news!" Gunner added. "Your cosmic landlords, the Stargazers and Starpower? Total narcs. They snitched on Airy's location."
From the fireplace, a voice spoke for the first time in a week. It was quiet, raspy from disuse, and dripping with a tired, bitter sarcasm that was achingly familiar.
"Well, huh," Airy said, not even turning his head. "Now I wonder why?????"
He slowly, deliberately, turned his head, his empty eyes finding Mym across the room and pinning him with a dead, accusatory glare.
"Maybe... was it because... oh, I don't know," Airy continued, his voice a low, venomous drawl, "someone kind of breaking into my lab and figuring something to happen?"
He held the look for a long, uncomfortable moment, letting the full weight of his blame settle on Mym. Then, with a weary sigh, he turned back to the crackling flames, pulling the blanket tighter around his shoulders.
"But oh well," he said to the fire, dismissing his own profound bitterness with a wave of his hand. "The past is the past. And now we should probably deal with what's going on in the present."
Finn and Gunner looked past Leo's shoulder, their cheerful expressions replaced with a flicker of genuine concern as they saw the hollowed-out state of their friend.
"He's taking this worse than the Burnt Universe thing," Gunner muttered to Finn, just loud enough for everyone to hear.

"Well, that's the thing," Finn said, rubbing his hands together for warmth. "They're lazy. Like, cosmically lazy. They don't like getting their own hands dirty, you know? A heavy, profound silence fell over the room as the implication sank in. Every member of Mym's team felt a familiar, cold dread creeping up their spines. They knew exactly who the biggest, meanest dog in this yard was.
From his place by the fire, Airy let out a single, harsh, bitter laugh. He didn't even turn around.
"Who the fuck else?" he spat into the flames, his voice a low, gravelly snarl of pure, contemptuous certainty. "Starpower and Stargazers don't like getting their hands dirty. So my guess is, for this world, it's OSD and Securly."
The names dropped into the quiet cabin like stones, shattering the fragile peace they had managed to build. OSD. Securly. The war was over. The Superintendent was gone. But the company, the corporation, the rotten, hydra-like entity that had caused all their pain... it still had heads.
As if summoned by the name, a new sound began to echo from the valley below the cabin. It was a low, rhythmic, thumping sound. It was faint at first, barely audible over the wind, but it was growing steadily louder.
Thump. Thump. Thump.

Airy's friends, Finn and Gunner, looked at each other, their cheerful, chaotic energy completely gone, replaced by a new, serious intensity. This wasn't a game anymore.
"Well," Gunner said, a grim smile on his face as he summoned a bizarre, cat-themed energy rifle into his hands. "Looks like the party found us."

He looked at Mym, a silent, bitter acknowledgment passing between them. You did this. You brought this to my door.
Then he turned to face the window, towards the sound of the approaching helicopters.
"Right," he said, his voice a low, tired whisper. "The past is the past."
The siege of Celeste Mountain had begun.
